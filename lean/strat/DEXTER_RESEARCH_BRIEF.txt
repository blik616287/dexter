DEXTER RESEARCH BRIEF
=====================

Dexter is our strongest card in terms of fire rate and reproducibility. It's a
compressed-volatility breakout signal -- it identifies stocks in a clear trend
(moving averages aligned and sloping together), waits for a period of low
volatility consolidation, then triggers when price breaks out of a 10-bar range
with above-average volume confirming the move. Volume is the confirmation gate,
not the driver -- the core thesis is that tight consolidations within an
established trend resolve as continuation breakouts, and elevated relative volume
validates the breakout as institutional rather than noise. It runs entirely on L1
market data (SMA, ATR, OHLCV) with no proxies or proprietary feeds, and the ATR
compression gate naturally favors mid/large cap equities whose ATR-to-price
ratio is structurally low.

Ensemble B (SPY scalp) also performed well in testing (100% win rate), but on
only 2 completed trades -- too small to validate. All 5 gates rely on proxy
approximations of proprietary data feeds (NYSE TICK, HIRO), and the proxy
quality is questionable (SAM returned the same value on every signal).
QuantConnect's data library does not carry the underlying feeds needed to
improve these proxies.

Index ETFs (SPY, QQQ) were excluded -- their intraday range is too narrow for
meaningful breakout trades, and their ATR ratio is so low that the compression
gate never filters, effectively reducing Dexter to a 3-gate model. Individual
equities with real intraday movement ($2-5/day) produce cleaner signals and
better per-trip P&L.

Selection criteria: market cap >$200B, average daily volume >10M shares,
ATR/close ratio structurally below the 0.8% compression threshold, sector
diversity, and sufficient intraday range for profitable breakout-to-EOD trades.
At 15 symbols with a 60-minute cooldown per symbol and signals concentrated in
the last 90 minutes of trading, realistic signal volume is 5-15 per day -- well
within the 20/hr alert limit. Fixing the RVOL calculation (simple to
time-of-day) before going live would tighten signal quality further by filtering
out false late-day volume spikes.
